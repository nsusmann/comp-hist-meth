Big Takeaways

  1. 01

    Everything is a dataset!

  2. 02

    You need to transform your sources into a functional dataset (this means you need to make a database).

    Transform your sources into a spreadsheet tutorial (PDF)
  3. 03

    Primary analytical and visualization modes:

    • Graphical
    • Spatial
  4. 04

    Be wary of making a “digital interface” (e.g., StoryMap) or public-facing website your end goal.

    You need to ask yourself: what's the best mechanism for disseminating the work? Is public dissemination my real goal?

Steps

  1. Acknowledge your larger research project/question.

    I am studying the sacred landscape of Profitis Ilias on Patmos, from the Bronze Age to the present day, with a key focus on the Monastery of Saint John the Theologian, which is located on its summit. How does the sacred catchment (spatial influence) of Saint John the Theologian’s Monastery on Patmos change depending on the historical period, and identity groups?

  2. Tease out a very specific research question.

    Is Saint John the Theologian represented in churches/monasteries/private chapels located off of Profitis Ilias?

  3. Push back: can computational analysis improve my ability to collect or manage the data, analyze it, or disseminate the work?

    Here’s an example of a text analysis interface I created to make my own writing process easier.

    You need to consider what modality best addresses your question and the limitations of your study (e.g., how much time you have to do it).
